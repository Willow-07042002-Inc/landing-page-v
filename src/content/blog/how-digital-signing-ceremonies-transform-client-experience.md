When remote online notarization became legal across more states, most estate planning attorneys saw it as a pandemic workaround. A stopgap. Something to do until the world went back to normal.

A few years later, many of those same attorneys are still running digital signing ceremonies — not because they have to, but because their clients prefer it.

Here's why.

## The ceremony itself doesn't have to happen in a conference room

For decades, the signing ceremony has meant one thing: everyone drives to your office, sits around a table, signs in the right places, and goes home. The notary stamps everything and the documents go into a folder.

That model works fine. But it has real costs — for clients who live an hour away, for elderly parents who don't drive, for adult children scattered across different states.

A well-run digital signing ceremony accomplishes the same thing. The attorney is there. The witnesses are there. The notary is there. Everyone can see each other. The documents are signed with legal force. It just happens over video, from wherever each person happens to be.

## Clients are more present when they're comfortable

There's a subtle thing that happens when clients come to a law office for a signing: some of them go into a passive mode. They've been asked to show up, they'll sign what you tell them to sign, and they want to get home for dinner.

That's not ideal for an estate plan. You want your clients to understand what they're signing.

When clients join a digital ceremony from their own home — sitting in a chair they recognize, maybe with a cup of coffee — they're often more relaxed, more present, and more willing to ask questions. The result is a better-informed client who's more confident in the work you did together.

## It opens the door to better document management

The signing ceremony is a natural moment to also solve the document storage problem.

After the ceremony, where do the documents go? Traditionally, the client gets a paper copy and a PDF by email, and the attorney keeps a file copy. That's the end of the relationship until someone needs an update.

Digital platforms built for estate planning can close the loop: documents are stored securely after signing, family members can be granted access, and the attorney maintains a connection to the client over time. The signing ceremony becomes the beginning of an ongoing relationship rather than the end of a transaction.

## What to look for in a signing platform

Not all digital signing tools are built for estate planning. A general-purpose e-signature platform can get you through a basic document, but it won't give you the ceremony structure that estate plans require — witnesses, notary, multiple signatories, the right legal workflow.

When evaluating platforms, look for:

- **RON (Remote Online Notarization) compliance** in your state
- **Multi-party video** that keeps everyone on screen together
- **Attorney-led flow** so you control the ceremony, not the software
- **Secure post-signing storage** with family access controls
- **Audit trail** that documents who signed what and when

The technology should disappear into the background. Your clients should just feel like they're doing something important with their attorney.

---

The estate planning industry is at an interesting moment. The tools to modernize exist, the legal framework supports them in most states, and clients increasingly expect a digital option. The firms that figure this out first will have a meaningful advantage.
