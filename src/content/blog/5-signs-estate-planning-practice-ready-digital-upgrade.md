Estate planning has always been one of the most personal areas of law. You sit across from families during some of the most consequential conversations of their lives, and the documents you draft carry weight far beyond the ink on the page.

But the *process* of creating, signing, and managing those documents? For most firms, that part still looks the same as it did twenty years ago.

Here are five signs your practice is ready for a change.

## 1. Your clients are calling to ask if their documents arrived

If your intake process involves mailing documents and then waiting for a signed copy to come back, you already know the drill: the phone call asking if you received it, the follow-up email, the scan that came through sideways.

Every one of those touchpoints is time your staff could spend on something that actually moves the work forward. When document delivery and signing happen digitally — with real-time status visibility — the calls stop.

## 2. Scheduling a signing ceremony takes multiple emails

Coordinating a time for clients to come in, getting family members on the calendar, rescheduling when someone's unavailable — for a lot of firms, this is a two-week process involving five emails and a phone tag.

Digital signing ceremonies change the math entirely. When clients can join from home and the ceremony is facilitated through a structured platform, you can often close a matter in days instead of weeks.

## 3. You have no easy way to share documents with the family after signing

Here's a question worth asking: what happens to your clients' estate documents after they leave your office?

For most families, the answer is "they go in a drawer somewhere." Years later, when those documents actually matter — when a spouse passes, or a child needs to locate the trust — there's a frantic search, and sometimes the documents are never found at all.

Practices that offer secure, family-accessible document storage don't just provide better service. They create a lasting relationship that drives referrals and repeat business.

## 4. Your intake process is still paper-based

If new clients are filling out paper questionnaires or emailing you their personal information through unencrypted channels, you're carrying risk you don't need to carry.

Digital intake also gives you structured data you can actually use. Instead of transcribing a handwritten form, you have information that flows cleanly into your document drafting process.

## 5. You're not capturing referrals from satisfied families

Estate planning clients who have a seamless, modern experience talk about it. But if your process ends when the documents are signed and there's no ongoing digital touchpoint, you're invisible when the conversation happens.

Firms that stay connected to their clients — through a secure portal where families can access documents and add beneficiaries — are the ones that get the call when a sibling needs to update their own will.

The good news: none of these are hard problems to solve. The tools exist. The question is whether you're ready to use them.
